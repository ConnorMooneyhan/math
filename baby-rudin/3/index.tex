\begin{ex}
  Prove that convergence of $\left\lbrace s_n \right\rbrace$ implies convergence of $\left\lbrace \left| s_n \right| \right\rbrace$.
  Is the converse true?
\end{ex}
\begin{proof}
  If $\left\lbrace s_n \right\rbrace \to s$, then let $\varepsilon > 0$ and $N \in \mathbb{Z}^+$ such that $n > N$ implies $|s_n - s| < \varepsilon$.
  Then \begin{align*}
    \big| |s_n| - |s| \big| \leq |s_n - s| < \varepsilon
  \end{align*}
  by the reverse triangle inequality.

  The converse hypothesis is contradicted by the alternating series $\left\lbrace (-1)^{n+1} \right\rbrace$, where the absolute value of the sequence clearly converges to $1$, whereas the sequence itself diverges.
\end{proof}
